# From role and story to playable rules

**Role contract.** For each selectable identity, tie a lived experience to a job in play. Describe the decision the role makes well, its counterplay, the passive trigger and visible feedback. In a peaceful game, a pilot might read wind while an engineer interprets equipment; give both actual input and consequences. In a combat game, define startup, active window, recovery, cost, cooldown, movement, effective range and hit volume for each ability. A projectile needs travel time, occlusion, an aimed direction and a miss condition. A parry needs a timed interval and frontal check. Balance unarmed baselines independently of cosmetics when equality matters.

**One source of truth.** Use stable role and action IDs for stats, collision, UI icon, keyboard binding, first-person pose, third-person rig and remote events. Keep local and remote appearances on the same skin/attachment map. Distinguish cast, hit, block, miss and cooldown in audio and imagery. Show an ability name while it recharges. Check one role against another at two distances and heights. In games with skins verify combinations using an asset matrix and inspect at least the most visually divergent cases in first and third person.

**Fiction and mission design.** Begin with an effect the player can observe. Invent causes, stakeholders and two defensible choices from the world's own constraints. For a commissioned quest, provide a giver, acceptance condition, discoverable physical evidence or actor, one action, a decision or obstacle, a recovery path, a consequence and a reward. Name a place only after you know how players reach its interaction point. Let discovering an offer, tracking it and accepting it be separate events. A handbook can show available, active and completed work; routes to another region point first to the correct exit. Conceal exact coordinates where discovery is the puzzle, but give an actionable clue.

A useful persisted record has a schema version, stable quest ID, phase, selected branch, unique objective IDs, reward claim and any world object the quest owns. Commit completion, stock/capacity changes and reward together. On replay a completed step cannot grant twice. Loading an older save should preserve its branch and let returning players experience new content. For a turn-based game the same state machine can track a negotiated treaty or a repaired supply line.

**Example of a new premise.** In a floating seed library, a failed lift strands two seed vaults at different temperatures. The player can move the fragile seeds in short cold trips or repair a slow conveyor before the vault warms. Deliver both options as interactions, show the changed vault contents and let later requests reflect the outcome. This illustrates a method; derive a different chain from each designer's own theme.

**Run a story, not just its scripts.** For games without commissions, play the relevant branches from the ordinary start and inspect the changed next turn, replay and recovery. For a world with discoverable errands, find the giver from the journal, refuse then accept, try an incorrect reading, switch tracked jobs, leave the area, reload, recover a lost object and deliver once. Test alternate branches and one locked reward when they exist.
